# Adversarial pass — ADR-0012, ADR-0013, ADR-0014

**Not yet. Two of the three decisions are sound in substance and defective in record; the third (ADR-0013) creates the repository's only control on the grading model without bounding, resourcing or instrumenting it, and `M-11` — the number the repo says outranks `M-09` — is not computable as specified.** What would change it: a defined audit population, a stated phase-1 sample rate and cadence (a range, per C-03, not a point), a named owner, and one metric definition for confirm-without-change.

The load-bearing claim across all three is not the one the brief names. It is **"the downgrade audit catches it."** R-12, R-13, ADR-0007 consequence 1, ADR-0008 consequence 3, ADR-0010 consequence 2, ADR-0012's second revisit condition, ADR-0014 consequence 2, and four user stories all discharge their residual risk into that one control. ADR-0013 is the document that had to make it real and is the thinnest of the three.

---

## 1. The audit is specified in five places and scoped in none of them the same way

**Evidence.** ADR-0013's decision: *"A sample of bulk dismissals and of the medium and low bands is pulled from the platform and read by a person."* Two populations. But R-13 requires *"Sample proposed-and-confirmed credit in the downgrade audit alongside downgrades."* ADR-0014 consequence 2 adds *"events where the manager confirmed the proposal without changing it."* US-006 declares *"confirmed-without-change is a population the downgrade audit can sample."* US-012 adds reclassified credit. M-11 defines the audit as *"bulk dismissals and of the medium and low bands"* only — matching ADR-0013 and contradicting R-13 and ADR-0014.

**Why it is wrong.** The ADR that owns the practice names the smallest population; the documents that depend on it have each written their own obligation into it by accretion. "The downgrade audit" means a different thing depending on which file you open, and the person holding the spreadsheet in week one has no single definition to work from. `scripts/validate.py` checks ID resolution, not semantic agreement between a risk's mitigation and the ADR it cites.

**Disposition: Survives.**

## 2. Deferring sample rate and cadence makes M-11's trend claim uncomputable

**Evidence.** M-11's target: *"a rising share of 'should have been higher' invalidates the grading model regardless of what M-09 says."* ADR-0013: *"Cadence and sample rate are deliberately not set here… a number invented now would be a false precision"*, citing C-03.

**Why it is wrong.** A *rising share* is a comparison across periods. With cadence and sample rate unset and explicitly floating on early findings, consecutive samples are drawn from different populations by different rules at different rates, and the series is not comparable to itself. The finding rate can rise because grading got worse or because the reader sampled more medium-band events this fortnight. C-03 requires a **range**, not silence — "50–450 events a day" is the canon's own worked example of the honest form. The ADR invokes C-03 to justify writing nothing, which is not what C-03 says.

**Disposition: Survives.** Cheap fix: state a range for both, and report the denominator with every finding rate.

## 3. ADR-0014's only revisit condition is a number no metric defines

**Evidence.** `metrics.yaml` contains one occurrence of "confirm": M-13's *"confirmed recognition actions per 100 credit-classified events"* — credit only. There is no metric for confirm-without-change across classes. ADR-0014 asserts *"[[M-13]] already needed that split for recognition; it now exists for all three classes"* — the register does not say that. And consequence 2 states the rate *"alone cannot tell you which"*.

**Why it is wrong.** The revisit condition is not instrumented, has no threshold, window or owner, and is declared uninterpretable in isolation by the same document three paragraphs earlier. Against `skills/decision-record.md` — *"a measurement, a date, a customer signal. Not 'if it becomes a problem'"* — this fails literally.

**Disposition: Survives.**

## 4. ADR-0014 was edited in place to reverse its own decision, and the reversed text still stands

**Evidence.** The Decision section: *"Confirming and acting are **one action, not two**."* The Correction section: *"Built, it was two: classify, then act."* Canon C-16: *"Decisions are records, not edits… Never edit a decision to match the present."* `skills/decision-record.md`: *"A reversal is a **new** ADR that supersedes the old one."*

**Why it is wrong.** Two defects. Procedural: an accepted ADR was amended in place rather than superseded, in a repository whose canon makes that a named violation — and one it otherwise observes scrupulously (ADR-0001 carries an unedited superseded banner; ADR-0010 amends ADR-0007 from its own document). Substantive: the correction did not remove the claim it corrects, so the document asserts both that confirming and acting are one action and that they are two. After the fix they are still two for risk and for credit.

**Disposition: Survives.** The honest form is ADR-0015 superseding ADR-0014.

## 5. ADR-0014 uses C-05 to kill the alternative and exempts itself from it

**Evidence.** *"Hide the proposal until the manager has classified it themselves… Rejected on [[canon#C-05]]: it adds a step to every event."* Consequence 1: *"A choice on every event where there was previously a default… [[canon#C-05]] means it has to be said out loud rather than absorbed."*

**Why it is wrong.** C-05 does not ask you to say the cost out loud; it asks what the change **removes**. The rejected alternative was refused for adding a step; the adopted decision adds a mandatory judgement to every event and names nothing removed. "Said out loud rather than absorbed" is a reassurance in the shape of a cost.

**Disposition: Survives narrowed.** C-05 is a queue rule and a per-event judgement is not a queue item — so this is a failure to discharge the ADR's own invocation of C-05, not proof the decision breaches it.

## 6. ADR-0012 reads A-12 one way for the scope it wants and the other way for the scope it cuts

**Evidence.** *"CameraMatics AI already detects vulnerable pedestrians, co-workers and cyclists ([[A-12]]). The capability is not a new build."* And: *"Bring in headway / tailgating… Rejected… Measuring following distance needs per-install camera calibration."* A-12's statement, in full, asserts *"detection of fatigue, distraction, smoking, phone use, **tailgating** and vulnerable road users"* — both capabilities, same confidence, same source.

**Why it is wrong.** A-12 is used as evidence that VRU detection is cheap because it already ships, and silently set aside for tailgating, which it says ships too. Either A-12 licenses "not a new build" — in which case the calibration objection must explain why the shipped tailgating detector does not count — or it does not, and the cheapness argument goes with it.

**Disposition: Survives.** One sentence repairs it: A-12 claims *presence*; it does not claim *distance*, and it does not distinguish them.

## 7. ADR-0012's cheapness and its cost rest on incompatible readings of the same assumption

**Evidence.** *"The capability is not a new build"* against consequence 1, *"a second camera-side workload competes for it… [[Q-03]] now has to answer for two models rather than one."*

**Why it is suspect.** If the model already runs in the product, the marginal cost is deployment reach, not a second workload. If it is a second workload on the target SoC, then for devices in scope it *is* a new build. Both hold only under a reading the ADR never states — that VRU detection ships on some device generations and not the ones v1 targets. That is exactly what Q-03 exists to settle, so the ADR rests a scope widening on a premise it also says is open.

**Disposition: Survives narrowed.**

## 8. ADR-0012's revisit condition depends on an audit that does not sample the thing it names

**Evidence.** *"Revisit when… The downgrade audit shows credit being proposed on events where the forward signal was absent or wrong."* ADR-0013's phase-1 practice samples bulk dismissals and the medium and low bands. Credit proposals are in neither, and M-11 does not include them.

**Why it is wrong.** A trigger for reopening a v1 scope decision fires off an observation the phase-1 control is not built to make. Finding 1 with teeth: the scoping disagreement disables a revisit condition in a different ADR.

**Disposition: Survives.**

## 9. ADR-0012 parks a live scope question in a review file, where no register can see it

**Evidence.** *"That is a **routing**-scope question… recorded as deferred in [[../../reviews/2026-09-14-prd-and-requirements]] rather than answered here."* `questions.yaml` holds Q-01 to Q-10 and none is this question.

**Why it is wrong.** The restraint is correct — C-25 item 2 forbids the agent closing it. But "deferred in a review document" is not a state the registers can hold. C-18 requires deliberate exclusions to be recorded as decisions, C-12 requires open risk to carry a question, and this carries neither. It is the single most likely thing in these three documents to be asked in an interview, and the repo's answer is a footnote.

**Disposition: Survives.** Fix is one `Q-11` entry, not a decision.

## 10. ADR-0013 declares its cost unpriced, assigns the pricing to phasing, and phasing does not carry it

**Evidence.** Consequence 1: *"An operational cost on our side, unpriced… the phasing section has to carry it."* `brain/phasing.md` mentions the audit twice and neither line carries headcount, time or any figure.

**Why it is wrong.** The ADR names an obligation and assigns it to a document that did not take it up. The bill, unpriced anywhere: a person reading a sample drawn from at least five populations (finding 1), at an unset rate (finding 2), across every live account, from first release, with a spreadsheet, until a phase-2 story that is deliberately unwritten.

**Disposition: Survives.** Note what does *not* survive: putting the work on CameraMatics rather than the fleet manager is correct, well argued, and the best judgement in the three documents. It keeps the control off the scarce resource — precisely the C-05 discipline finding 5 says ADR-0014 skipped.

## 11. Two of three revisit conditions are judgements, not observable states

**Evidence.** ADR-0013: *"The manual sample stops being readable in the time available"* — conceded in consequence 4: *"Phase 2's trigger is a judgement, not a metric."* ADR-0012: *"a materially smaller share of the installed base"* — unquantified, against a baseline that is itself unmeasured. ADR-0014: finding 3.

**Why it is only half wrong.** ADR-0013's is honest rather than evasive and names who would call it. But with cadence unset, "the time available" has no baseline to be exceeded. ADR-0012's is repairable the moment Q-03 returns a number.

**Disposition: Survives narrowed.**

## 13. A-19 does not say what ADR-0013 uses it to say

**Evidence.** *"[Cadence and sample rate] depend on event volume ([[A-19]], unmeasured)."* A-19 is an assertion, not a gap: *"This is the working assumption for design purposes,"* confidence medium, source *"Danny's direction for this exercise."* The unmeasured-ness lives in **A-02** and Q-02.

**Disposition: Survives narrowed.** Cite A-02 alongside A-19.

## What I would sign, and what I would send back

**Sign: ADR-0012 part 2** — *"Every event carries every sensor input available at the time, regardless of which signal triggered it."* The best decision in the three. Genuinely free, correctly identified as the durable half, holds whatever Q-03 answers, already propagated into `stack-outline.md` coherently, and the trigger/signal distinction is the actual defect in ADR-0001 rather than a scope preference dressed up. It survives every attack I made on it.

**Send back: ADR-0012 part 1** — not the decision, which is defensible, but findings 6, 7 and 9. Three sentences fix it.

**Send back: ADR-0013**, hardest of the three. The judgement is right — separate practice from tooling, put it on CameraMatics, refuse to invent a number — and the execution leaves the repo's only control with no defined population, no comparable measurement, no price and no owner beyond a company name. ADR-0008 asked for an owner: *"A control nobody performs is not a control."* ADR-0013 answers "CameraMatics", which is an organisation, not an owner. A control assigned to a company is the same shape as the gap it was written to close.

**Send back: ADR-0014**, on the record rather than the reasoning. The substance survives and should be defended: the neutral argument is the sharpest single insight in the three documents, the anti-anchoring alternative is engaged at its strongest, and the J-27 overrule is correctly resolved against the agent's own proposal. What must change: supersede rather than edit, strike the "one action, not two" clause, define confirm-without-change or the revisit condition is inert, drop R-12, and do the C-05 subtraction rather than announce the cost.

**Do the three together survive C-05?** Yes, narrowly, and not for the reason the documents give. ADR-0013 deliberately spends CameraMatics attention rather than manager attention — that is the move that makes the sum work. ADR-0012 adds evidence per event, not items to the queue. ADR-0014 is the only one that adds to what a human must do, and the only one that names nothing removed. The set passes because of ADR-0013's design, which is why finding 10 matters: the cost was moved, not eliminated, and it is on a line nobody has written down.

**C-25 check.** No breach found. ADR-0012 widens scope and it is ratified in J-21 with the agent's contribution and omission both recorded — the process working. No invented numbers in any of the three.
