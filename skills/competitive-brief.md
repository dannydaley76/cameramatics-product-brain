## When to use
When evaluating a competitor or competitive threat, or before launching a
feature where the landscape changes the decision. Output should inform product
decisions, not document what exists.

## Format

### Competitive brief: [competitor or feature area]

**Why we're looking at this**
What decision or context triggered it? What do we need to know?

**Who they are**
One paragraph. What the product is, who uses it, the core value prop.

**What they do well**
3–5 specific things, evidence-based where possible. Don't undersell a strong
competitor — a flattering brief is a useless one.

**Where they fall short**
3–5 specific gaps.

**Their pricing model**
How they charge, who pays, the entry point.

**What this means for us**
The "so what". Match, differentiate, or ignore — and what specifically changes
as a result.

**Open questions**
What we still don't know that matters.

## Conventions
- Actionable over interesting. "They have feature X" is useless without "and
  here's what we do about it".
- Real user feedback (G2, app stores, forums) over marketing copy.
- Update when something material changes, not on a schedule.
- Parity is context, never a reason ([[../brain/canon]] C-08).
